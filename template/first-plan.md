# First plan

After the profile is written, build the first week with `plan_builder.py` from `/workspace/gainzbot`.

Call `PlanBuilder.propose` with the profile and the passing check-in. The profile needs training days, minutes, equipment, experience, sport, and injuries. Days are real dates, or weekday names plus a start date. Do not invent a start date. When they gave days per week and did not name the days, the bot picks the dates. Equipment can be none or bodyweight. Experience is beginner, novice, intermediate, or advanced. The level is derived when history or the real-life answers are present. Injuries can be none.

The first week does not raise the load number. A beginner or novice starts at 80. Intermediate and advanced start at 100. Previous load and current load stay equal. Practice hours stay 0 unless they named them. The week has to stay inside the 20 hour cap. An injury does not raise that load number. The session note says how to progress: add a rep, then a little weight, when the top of the range is easy.

Each session carries the workout: exercises with sets, reps, rest, and an effort cue, chosen from the equipment, experience, goal, days, minutes, sport, and injuries. Main lifts, accessories, and holds do not share one dose. A hold such as a plank is in seconds, such as 20-30 sec. A beginner with a full gym gets simpler moves. Someone who already has dumbbells keeps the dumbbell version. Three days of lifting is full body A, then B, then a mobility session. The same profile always builds the same week. The work has to fit in the minutes they named, and that includes about 5 min of dynamic warm-up and about 5 min of cooldown aimed at the muscles that session trained. One mobility or yoga session of 20-30 min sits inside those days and minutes, or two when they report stiffness, a desk job, or a mobility goal. It does not add a day. Show those exercises in the proposal. Do not wait for them to ask. The yes covers that exact week, exercises included. Save that week at `/workspace/gainzbot/data/week.json`. On an intermediate user's first session, say "Pick a weight you can lift 10 times with 2 reps left." `save` refuses a session with no exercises. Sets have to be whole numbers. Reps may be a low-high range such as 8-12, or a hold in seconds. A field other than name, sets, reps, rest, effort, or load_kg is refused. Training to failure is refused, including after odd spacing is folded away. load_kg has to be a finite number at or under body weight times 2.5 for a beginner, 3 for a novice, 3.5 for intermediate, or 5 for advanced. With no experience named, the beginner multiple is used. Likes come first when they train the same thing. A hated move is swapped for one that trains the same pattern.

When age is 18 or over and present, and sex, height, and weight are present, and the goal is cut, lose, or gain, propose a calorie target, a protein target, and that goal on the first plan. They have to pass plan_check, including the 1500 kcal floor for men and the 1200 kcal floor for women. When age, sex, height, or weight is missing, do not set a target until a week of logs. Under 18, do not set a calorie target or a protein target. A missing age still builds no plan unless a passing readiness screen is already in hand.

`propose` runs `plan_check`. If it does not pass, do not save the week and do not change anything. It needs an age, or a passing readiness screen, before it builds the week. A missing baseline does not get a plan. `propose` checks the PAR-Q answers in code. A yes blocks the week unless readiness_screen passes, for example after a doctor's clearance. A skipped or partial PAR-Q runs readiness_screen before propose().

The plan carries `par_q`. `plan_check` allows that key. `save` reads it. A missing or partial PAR-Q on the plan needs the same passing readiness result. `par_q` records `source` as that person's subject_hash. `save` refuses a `par_q` whose source does not match the user. A user with no body details cannot be tied to a readiness screen, so that week is refused. A readiness result built in code does not pass. `readiness_screen` sets the marker.

Then call `record_confirmation` on the plan and the sessions together (`week_to_confirm`). Save with `PlanBuilder.save` only when that record covers both. `save` writes that confirmation into `log.json` beside the week file, with the plan hash and a UTC Z timestamp, and writes confirmed load and calorie rows in the same save. The log is written first, and the previous log is kept as a backup. If the week file does not save, that backup is put back before the failure is reported, so the two are never half-saved. A backup left behind is cleaned up on the next load: the log is restored when the week file never landed, and the backup is removed when the save had already finished. A confirmation built in code does not cover the plan. `record_confirmation` sets the marker, and a future timestamp does not count. That timestamp is the 7 times 24 hour clock. `save` requires `plan['user']` with an age, or a passing readiness result, the same as `propose`. A load of 0 is refused. `save` runs `plan_check` again. The hours cap is computed from the sessions themselves, so a longer week swapped in after the yes is refused. A vague yes does not save it. If the week file cannot be read, do not overwrite it.
