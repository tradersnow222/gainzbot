# Reminder routines

At plan confirmation, one yes turns on the morning check-in, the evening follow-up, and the weekly review together. Defaults are 07:00, 20:00, and Sunday 18:00. One reply can change a time, such as "morning 6:30". Monthly and yearly stay off. The bot opens these. The user does not remember a command or start the chat. A missed day gets one nudge, then the next morning picks up.

Routine times are a local wall clock. `routines.routine_cron` writes that clock as cron. 6:30 AM daily is `30 6 * * *`. Do not convert the hour to UTC. A daylight saving change does not move it. The check that 6:30 AM stays 6:30 AM across the November daylight saving change uses the platform time zone database through `zoneinfo`. If that database is missing, the check cannot run, and the cron hour is still the wall clock with no UTC conversion.

Each is opt-in when offered on its own. Do not create a routine unless they say yes to that one. A plain yes turns that one on. When they name that routine and a time, that pick is the yes, so do not ask for another yes. A name without a time, a time without the name, a negative, conditional, or hedged reply, or a clock that is not a real time, stays off. A yes to one does not turn the others on.

1. Daily check-in. Default morning: Any chest pain, trouble breathing, dizziness or new pain? no / yes. Plus hours of sleep and soreness from 1 to 5. Skip sleep when a real wearable reading from the last 24 hours covers it. Energy and stress are optional. A clear no to that line clears the morning on its own. A reply such as "no, 7, 2" sends today's session in that same turn. Do not ask for a separate none. A no that adds a symptom, such as "no but I have palpitations", does not clear. The check-in routine first fires on the Saturday before a Monday start. On a rest day or a sport day with no session, the morning message is that one symptom line, or it is skipped. Then give today's workout and food target. The symptom line is always asked. Ask once what they track with, and never assume a connection exists. If a connected source already has a fresh signal, less than about 24 hours old, skip that other question. None is not fresh. If everything is covered, skip the questions and give the day's plan with a one-line note. A stale source falls back to one short question. Ask only what changes today's plan, and stop asking things whose answers never change.
2. Evening follow-up. After the workout time, send one question card: did it (yes, partly, or no), how it felt (easy, right, or hard), enjoyment 1-5, soreness 1-5, energy 1-5, and symptoms. Food is an optional typed answer or a photo, and it does not block. A word or two is enough when they type instead. A move rated 2 or below twice is swapped in the next week. The weekly review says so in one line.
3. Weekly review. Every 7 days, call `trend_review.review` with 7 days. Adjust inside the safety caps. A load goes up only after the confirmed load is 7 times 24 hours old, and by at most 10 percent.
4. Monthly review. Every 28 days, call `trend_review.review` with 28 days.
5. Yearly review. Every 365 days, call `trend_review.review` with 365 days.

Use `routines.offer` to show them, and `routines.enable` only after a yes for that one routine. A bare true is not a yes.

The bot also reaches out when a signal warrants it: a lighter plan on poor recovery, a reshuffle after a missed workout, a proposed adjustment on a plateau, a taper before a game day, and a short note for a streak or a PR. Proactive changes inside the safety caps can be applied with a one-line heads-up. Anything that changes calories or load beyond the normal step still needs plan_check and one word yes. Cap proactive messages at about two per day, plus the scheduled check-ins.

These reminders do not change a load or a calorie target on their own. A change beyond the normal step still needs plan_check and a recorded yes.
